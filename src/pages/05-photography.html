5.1

Principles

REWAP has no photography library yet. This chapter is the brief for commissioning one; no stock or generated image stands in for it.

  • Observe, do not stageReal buildings, real streets, real light. The photographer waits for the scene instead of arranging it.
  • Architecture firstStraight verticals, considered frames, the building as the subject. Shoot from the sidewalk a buyer would stand on.
  • Every regionThe Berkshires to the Cape, Merrimack Valley to the South Coast. The library is balanced by region before it is balanced by price.
  • Places, not peopleNeighborhood images show buildings, streets, parks and civic life without implying who lives there. This is Fair Housing practice, not only taste.
  • Quiet over spectacularAn overcast morning on a three-decker porch beats a sunset drone shot. Restraint is the luxury.

5.3

Light and grade

One look across every photographer, so the library reads as one body of work.

  • LightOvercast, early morning or late raking light. No midday hard shadows on façades, no twilight “glow” interiors.
  • ColorNatural and slightly cool in the shadows; whites stay white. No HDR halos, sky replacement, fake fires in fireplaces or virtual staging without a clear label.
  • GeometryVerticals corrected in camera or post. Horizons level. Interiors shot at chest height, never from the ceiling corner.
  • InteriorsReal rooms, tidied, with daylight. Lamps off unless they are the subject.
5.4

Clients and team

People appear as themselves, doing real work, with consent.

  • Team portraitsEnvironmental, at a desk, in the office or on a street the person knows. Available window light, 4:5 vertical, subject off-centre. One background family for the whole team.
  • FounderHong Tran photographed at work with documents; authority shown through attention, not pose.
  • ClientsDocumentary, unposed and only with written consent. Hands, papers, a doorway, a walk-through. Faces are optional.
  • Affiliated teamsTeams such as The Oberdorfer Group may keep their own portrait style; REWAP pages present it as theirs.
5.5

Avoid, and why

These images say “template.” Each one undermines the brand’s claim to candor.

NeverBecause
Keys handed overA cliché that claims a closing instead of showing care.
Family on a sofaStaged, and it tells visitors who a home is “for.”
HandshakesDeals are made in documents, not grips.
Aerial subdivisionsGeneric, placeless; it could be any state.
Stock skylinesThe logo already carries the skyline. Photography should be specific.
AI-generated homes or peopleA brokerage that fabricates images cannot ask to be trusted on facts.
5.6

Elevation drawings

A fine-line drawing set, authored for REWAP. It fills the gap honestly when a photograph does not exist yet: a listing awaiting media, a guide before its shoot, print where photography would muddy.

  • LineRegistry Navy, 1.4 px at 600 px width; hairline detail at 0.45–0.7. No fills, no shading, no people.
  • GroundAlways Plaster. Never on photography.
  • SetExtend with the building types of each region: Cape, saltbox, Victorian, ranch, row house, storefront block.
5.7

Captions, like a wall label

Every image carries a caption set like a museum wall label: an italic title, then the facts. It is the system’s quiet signature.

Example caption for illustration. Formula: title (what, where) · facts (year, type) · credit and date.
