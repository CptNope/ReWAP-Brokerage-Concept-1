1.2

The name, as written

The origin of “REWAP” has not been published, and this book does not invent one. What the name does carry is visible in the mark itself.

In the logo the first two letters stand in bronze before a navy W. Read that way, the name opens with RE, the trade’s own shorthand for real estate, and turns on the one letter the mark chose to make larger, darker and home to a house. That is a reading of the artwork, not a history of the company, and it should be used only in that spirit.

Treat REWAP as a proper name. Set it in capitals in running text, never expand it as an acronym, never add punctuation (R.E.W.A.P.), and always follow it with “Brokerage” on first reference: REWAP Brokerage. The legal name, REWAP Brokerage LLC, belongs in disclosures, contracts and the footer.

1.3

Three audiences, equal weight

Buyers, sellers and investors are served in parallel. No page leads with one at the expense of the others; each has its own path from the first screen.

Buyers
Often searching on a phone in the evening, comparing towns as much as houses. They need to understand what an offer commits them to before they make it, and they need search that respects their time. What they should feel: prepared.
Sellers
Deciding whom to trust with a price, a timeline and the legal side of a sale. They are judging judgment. What they should feel: represented by someone who has read every line.
Investors
Evaluating rental, multi-family and value-add property anywhere in the Commonwealth. They want numbers that are sourced, terms that are explained and no adjectives. What they should feel: informed enough to move quickly.
Agents & teams
Brokers, agents and affiliated groups such as The Oberdorfer Group, who operate under REWAP and keep their own names. They need a brokerage that frames their work without absorbing it.

1.6

Values

How the firm behaves when no one is checking. Each value has a test.

Clarity
Every client can explain, in their own words, what they have agreed to. Test: could the client summarise this document back to us?
Diligence
The fine print is read before it is signed, by someone qualified to read it. Test: has anything in this file gone unreviewed?
Candor
Advice serves the client’s position, including when the advice is to wait or walk away. Test: would we say this if it cost us the commission?
Stewardship
A client relationship is measured in years and properties, not in one closing. Test: will this decision still look right in five years?
Equal standing
Every region, property type and client budget receives the same standard of care. Test: would we describe this town, or this client, differently if it were wealthier?

1.8

Emotional objective

From uncertainty to composure.

The feeling of having read the fine print, and understood it. A visitor should leave any REWAP page calmer and better informed than when they arrived, whether or not they contact the firm.

1.9

Voice

Plain, exact and unhurried. REWAP writes the way a good attorney explains a contract to a client they respect: no jargon left undefined, no adjective doing a number’s job.

  • Lead with the answerState the conclusion first, then the reasoning. Headlines are statements, not teasers.
  • Define, then useTerms such as “contingency,” “earnest money” or “title insurance” are explained in a clause the first time they appear on a page.
  • Numbers over adjectives“1,840 square feet, built 1912” rather than “spacious historic home.” If a number is unknown, say so.
  • Describe places, not peopleFair Housing applies to every word. Describe properties, streets and amenities; never the people who live, or should live, there.
  • Short sentences, complete thoughtsOne idea per sentence. No exclamation marks. No ellipses.
